## Reported: a compact evidence record

[ZeRO v3, §§3–7](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.02054v3#S3) analyzes partitioning optimiser states, gradients and parameters. Its contribution is distributed ownership/scheduling rather than a new optimisation objective. **§10 evaluates ZeRO-100B: optimiser-plus-gradient partitioning with ZeRO-R**, sometimes combined with Megatron model parallelism. It does not isolate complete parameter-partitioned stage 3 experimentally.

[§§10.1–10.2 and Figure 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.02054v3#S10) report 400 V100 GPUs in 25 DGX-2 nodes with 800 Gbps internode bandwidth, models up to 170B parameters, and speedups up to 10× against the stated baselines. §10.5/Figures 6–8 compare Table 3's optimisation bundles; the appendix records differing parallelism and batches. These conditional systems results establish neither universal speedup nor matched convergence.

The [submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.02054v3) lists withdrawn v1 on 4 October 2019, v2 on 7 October, and reviewed v3 on 13 May 2020. The review date above is separate.

## Derived: ownership changes the denominator

The accompanying [memory lesson](/blog/model-development-training-memory/) makes one illustrative layout explicit: two-byte working weights, two-byte gradients, four-byte master weights and eight bytes of Adam moments. The following is educational arithmetic under those assumptions, not a reconstructed allocation trace from the paper.

Let $N$ count trainable scalars and $d$ count ranks in the sharding group. If a state has $c$ bytes per scalar, replication costs $cN$ per rank while ideal even partitioning costs $cN/d$. Applying this operation to selected ledger rows gives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_0&=16N, & M_1&=4N+12N/d,\\
M_2&=2N+14N/d, & M_3&=16N/d.
\end{aligned}
$$

One useful independent question is what happens to **aggregate** memory. Multiplying by $d$ gives $16Nd$, $4Nd+12N$, $2Nd+14N$ and $16N$. Increasing the group therefore reduces the redundancy of partitioned states while leaving replicated terms multiplied across ranks. This explains why adding devices can change model capacity before considering faster computation.

It also exposes an asymptote. Under this layout, stage 2 can never bring per-rank state below $2N$ merely by increasing $d$. For seven billion parameters, that surviving term is fourteen decimal gigabytes. A proposed device budget below it contradicts the assumptions before any benchmark is needed.

None of these equations includes activation memory or the extra parameters materialized during computation. A full-state partition is an ownership statement; a kernel still needs its operands. The distinction is the bridge from paper analysis to an allocation timeline.

## Analysis: capacity and speed need separate explanations

An independent interpretation is that **more capacity can enable a different execution regime**. Once a job has room for a larger microbatch, useful work per kernel may change. Once it requires fewer model-parallel partitions, its communication pattern may change. Those are plausible causal pathways to investigate; they must not be relabeled as isolated measurements of the state-partitioning mechanism.

For a fair follow-up comparison, define the estimand first. Are we measuring the best throughput achievable within a fixed device budget, or the effect of a particular technique with workload and schedule held constant? Both questions are useful, but their controls differ. A best-configuration comparison permits tuning; an isolated intervention needs matched conditions.

Likewise, tokens per second does not alone establish time to a target validation loss. A changed effective batch can alter the number and character of updates. A convergence claim needs the data stream, token budget, optimiser settings, stopping criterion and evaluation protocol. This review supplies no independent measurement of those quantities.

## Unknown: what should be measured before adoption?

The practical unknown is the complete behavior of **your intended configuration**. The historical result does not specify its peak, network contention, checkpoint behavior or recovery cost. Current software documentation must be checked separately: the [DeepSpeed stage and memory controls](https://deepspeed.readthedocs.io/en/latest/zero3.html) and [PyTorch FSDP2 tutorial](https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/intermediate/FSDP_tutorial.html) are implementation references, rather than additional original-paper experimental evidence.

A proposed follow-up would hold model, sequence length, effective batch, precision and hardware fixed, then record state allocations, peak allocated/reserved memory, step time and collective time for each viable policy. Report failures as well as successful runs. If a policy permits a larger microbatch, add a separately labeled tuned comparison rather than folding it into the controlled one.

To isolate a materialization effect, change only grouping or prefetching and inspect the allocation timeline. To isolate activation pressure, change only microbatch or recomputation. These interventions test competing explanations for a peak; neither is claimed to have been run here.
