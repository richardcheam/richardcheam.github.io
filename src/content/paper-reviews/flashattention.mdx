## The problem and what came before

Attention's cost grows with the square of the sequence length $N$, and by 2022 many methods approximated it, with sparse or low-rank structure, to reduce its FLOPs. The paper's opening observation is that many of these "do not display wall-clock speedup against standard attention" (Sec 1, p. 1). The reason, it argues, is that attention on a GPU is limited by memory traffic, not arithmetic: standard implementations write the $N \times N$ score matrix to high-bandwidth memory (HBM), read it back for softmax, write the probabilities and read them again. Reducing FLOPs does not help an operation that is waiting for memory.

## The method

The algorithm (Sec 3.1, Algorithm 1, pp. 4–5) splits $Q$, $K$ and $V$ into blocks sized to fit in on-chip SRAM. For each block of keys and values, it loads the block, computes scores against a block of queries on chip, and updates the output for those queries. Softmax normalization is handled with running statistics: for each query, the maximum score $m$ seen so far and the running sum $\ell$ of exponentials, with previously accumulated output rescaled by $e^{m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}}}$ when the maximum grows. The result is exact softmax attention, up to floating-point rounding. The [efficient-execution post](/blog/model-development-efficient-execution/#attention-without-the-score-matrix) works the rescaling through on four scores.

The backward pass needs the attention probabilities. Instead of storing them, FlashAttention stores only the output and the statistics $(m, \ell)$ and recomputes the probabilities block by block during backward (Sec 3.1, p. 5; App. B). This adds FLOPs but removes the $O(N^2)$ memory.

**The IO analysis** is what makes the paper more than a fast kernel. With head dimension $d$ and SRAM of size $M$ (with $d \le M \le Nd$), standard attention needs $\Theta(Nd + N^2)$ HBM accesses and FlashAttention $\Theta(N^2 d^2 / M)$ (Theorem 2, p. 6). For typical $d$ of 64 to 128 and SRAM of about 100 KB, $d^2/M$ is well below 1, so FlashAttention needs many times fewer accesses. A lower bound shows no exact attention algorithm can asymptotically do better over all SRAM sizes (Proposition 3, p. 6). The paper also extends the method to block-sparse attention (Sec 3.3).

## The decisive evidence

The most informative result is the smallest. For GPT-2 medium's attention (sequence 1,024, head dimension 64, 16 heads, batch 64) on an A100 (Fig. 2, p. 6):

| | GFLOPs | HBM reads and writes | Runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard attention | 66.6 | 40.3 GB | 41.7 ms |
| FlashAttention | 75.2 | 4.4 GB | 7.3 ms |

FlashAttention does more arithmetic, because of recomputation, yet runs 5.7 times faster, tracking the 9-fold reduction in memory traffic. That single comparison supports the paper's thesis directly. The figure's middle panel shows runtime falling with block size until the operation becomes compute-bound, again as the IO analysis predicts.

End to end (Sec 4.1):

- **BERT-large** to MLPerf's target accuracy: 17.4 ± 1.4 minutes against 20.0 ± 1.5 for NVIDIA's MLPerf 1.1 record implementation, about 15% faster, over 10 runs on 8 A100s (Table 1, p. 7).
- **GPT-2** on OpenWebText, at equal perplexity: GPT-2 small in 2.7 days against 4.7 for Megatron-LM and 9.5 for Hugging Face; GPT-2 medium in 6.9 days against 11.5 and 21.0 (Table 2, p. 8). That is 3.0–3.5 times faster than Hugging Face and 1.7 times faster than Megatron-LM.
- **Longer context:** GPT-2 small trained with 4 times the context in 3.6 days, still faster than Megatron-LM at 1K, and 0.7 better in perplexity (Table 4, p. 8).

## What the evidence supports, confounds and leaves open

**Supported.** Exact attention can be made several times faster and linear in memory by scheduling alone. The kernel-level measurement (Fig. 2) isolates that claim cleanly: same computation, same hardware, traffic and time measured. The IO analysis explains why, and the lower bound says the approach is close to the limit.

**Confounded by the baseline.** End-to-end speedups depend on what is being replaced. Against Hugging Face's 2022 GPT-2, FlashAttention is 3–3.5 times faster; against Megatron-LM, already heavily optimized, 1.7 times. The text also states "1.8× over Megatron" in one summary (Sec 4, p. 7) while the table gives 1.74 and 1.67, a small inconsistency worth noticing. Part of each end-to-end gain comes from fusing other operations into the kernel and from larger batches that the memory savings allow, not only from attention's IO.

**Context-dependent.** The constants depend on the GPU's SRAM size, HBM bandwidth and head dimension. Later GPUs and later versions of the kernel changed the absolute numbers considerably; a 2022 A100 measurement says little about speed on today's hardware, though the reasoning carries over.

**Left open.** Numerical behaviour: the algorithm is exact in real arithmetic, but block-wise accumulation changes the order of floating-point operations, so outputs are not bitwise identical to a standard implementation; the paper does not study this. The longer-context quality gains (Path-X, long-document classification) show what becomes possible, not that longer context always helps.

## How it changes decisions

- Use a fused, IO-aware attention kernel by default for training and long-context inference; in PyTorch, `scaled_dot_product_attention` selects one where available.
- When an operation is slow, measure memory traffic and arithmetic intensity before reducing FLOPs; approximations that save FLOPs may not save time.
- Treat activation memory for attention as linear in sequence length when such kernels are used, which changes [recomputation and parallelism choices](/blog/model-development-sharding-recomputation/).
- Re-benchmark on your own hardware and baseline rather than transferring a published speedup.
